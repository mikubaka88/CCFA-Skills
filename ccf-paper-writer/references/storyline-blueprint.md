# Storyline Blueprint

Use this for full-manuscript organization, substantial introductions, or explicitly requested storyline design. Ordinary polishing and bounded abstracts use the writer entry's local argument.

## Start From The Scientific Content

A storyline explains the research question, what makes it difficult, the idea that changes the approach, and what the work establishes. Its organization follows the contribution. A theoretical result, dataset, system, and empirical diagnosis need different explanations; several related contributions can belong to one paper.

Before drafting, recover the relevant propositions from the supplied materials:

- What phenomenon, task, or question is studied, and why does it matter?
- What does the closest work already explain or enable?
- Which specific assumption, information constraint, or unresolved relationship motivates this work?
- What does the proposed method compute, represent, optimize, or construct?
- How does that change address the question?
- What do the supplied observations, analysis, proof, or construction establish?

These are thinking prompts, not mandatory headings or a fixed paragraph sequence. Keep a hypothesis in its proposed stage. Do not invent a failure of existing models to create motivation or infer a root cause from a lower benchmark score alone. Writing preserves the authorized research; a missing mechanism belongs to its owner rather than being supplied by eloquent prose.

For new manuscripts, `materials-to-manuscript.md` controls pending results and content before page fitting. A planned comparison does not establish an effect.

## Compose Before Checking

Choose the scientific message of the next paragraph and the supplied facts or reasoning that develop it. Use the research object as the subject and state its operation, property, or observed behavior. Introduce technical terms where the reader needs them, then carry them through the explanation.

For example, explaining a representation may require its contents, what distinctions it retains, and how the next computation uses those distinctions. Describing an experiment may require the comparison, observed change, and competing interpretation it resolves. A label for the quality of the research does not supply an operation or relationship.

When learning expression from a selected paper, follow `exemplar-style-analysis.md`: inspect a relevant source passage and transfer its sentence relationships before composing. A source's paragraph count, contribution list, or confidence does not become a requirement for the new manuscript.

Keep internal support checks separate from the writing plan. The plan says what the reader will understand; the check asks whether each assertion has a basis. Neither requires a visible process report.

## Organize The Contribution

### Problem And Prior Work

Explain the actual setting and what the closest approaches do. Use their relevant assumptions or operations to explain an unresolved issue. “Few papers study X” identifies coverage, not why X is difficult. “Existing methods perform poorly” needs the specific behavior and its conditions before it can motivate a mechanism.

Present prior work accurately even when it does not fit a neat progression toward the new method. Distinguish a source's stated result from the authors' interpretation. Use `citation-workflow.md` for unresolved source support.

### Insight And Method

Explain the changed relationship or formulation before naming all its parts. A reader should be able to describe the idea without reciting module names.

Organize the method around the dependencies needed to understand it:

- Define inputs, outputs, and the mathematical or computational object.
- Explain the operation and the information on which it depends.
- State the reason for that operation and its necessary assumptions.
- Develop equations, constructions, or algorithmic steps at the point where they answer the question.
- Distinguish a design rationale from an effect established by supplied results.

Use module boundaries when they reflect the method. Do not impose an input/operation/output form on every paragraph or split a connected derivation to fill a module list. A non-model contribution may instead need definitions, a proof roadmap, a measurement argument, or an explanation of dataset construction.

### Results And Interpretation

Order the actual findings by the questions they answer. The applicable sequence might explain an observed failure, compare the proposed change, and examine why it works. It may instead follow a theorem and its consequences or a resource and the behaviors it measures. No category is mandatory merely because it appears in a template.

For each result passage, recover:

- The scientific question and relevant comparison or assumptions.
- The actual observation or derived statement.
- Its interpretation and any alternative explanation the result leaves open.
- A material condition that changes that interpretation.

Write these relationships directly. State the comparison itself and distinguish the measured effect of an ablation from the full causal explanation. Preserve negative findings and necessary uncertainty. Add no experiments solely to complete a narrative sequence.

### Conclusions

State what the work establishes and why it matters in the studied setting. Preserve connected method, measurement, and task contributions. Do not turn an empirical observation into a universal principle or force a caveat at the end. Future work is useful only when it contributes a specific unresolved question.

## Section Responsibilities

| Section | What the reader should understand |
| --- | --- |
| Abstract | The question, contribution, essential operation or idea, and supported finding. |
| Introduction | Why the question matters, what remains unresolved, and how the contribution addresses it. |
| Related Work | The relevant approaches, assumptions, and closest relationships to the present work. |
| Method / Theory / Resource | The definitions, operations, assumptions, and reasoning needed to understand the contribution. |
| Experiments / Analysis | What was compared or derived, what was found, and how it changes the interpretation. |
| Figures and Tables | The quantities, relationships, or behavior relevant to the accompanying scientific point. |
| Limitations, when applicable | Material conditions, observed failures, or required disclosures and their consequences. |
| Conclusion | The established result and its scientific consequence. |

Use the source's section names and the user's requested structure. Section roles do not impose a model-paper outline on theory, systems, data, or study papers.

## Requested Storyline Comparisons

Compare substantially different storylines only when comparison or redesign is requested or a material organizational choice remains. Preserve the supplied method, results, and constraints. Reframing is not authorization to invent a stronger contribution.

Each candidate should identify the main question, explanatory starting point, relationship between contributions, proposed section order, and what would become easier or harder to understand. Select perspectives that resolve this choice, such as problem framing, mechanism explanation, or interpretation of results; no fixed expert panel is required. Label actual independent contributions and single-agent perspectives accurately.

Compare candidates against the same supplied materials:

- Does the framing express the whole contribution?
- Does the explanation connect the problem and the actual operation?
- Can a reader follow the reasoning without missing definitions or forced transitions?
- Does the framing preserve the strength and scope of the results?
- Does it introduce unsupported novelty, hidden assumptions, or a different research objective?

Select or combine candidates for a concrete reason. Use actual review findings when available; do not simulate a hostile reviewer to create extra limitations. A stylistic alternative does not reopen the research direction.

## Check The Draft Once

After composition, reconstruct the paragraph messages briefly from the actual text. Repair a missing reason or relation from supplied material, merge repetition, and remove sentences with no scientific function. A transition word cannot create a missing argument.

Check affected dependencies:

1. Major assertions have applicable source, result, derivation, or design support.
2. Proposed mechanisms, planned tests, and observed findings remain distinct.
3. Terms, variables, quantities, comparison direction, and assumptions are consistent across sections.
4. The abstract and conclusion express the work actually described.
5. Material negative results and uncertainties remain visible where they change interpretation.

Keep this check internal unless a diagnosis or rationale is requested. Reuse unchanged verification and review findings. An unresolved research decision affects its dependent passage; continue supported writing without a repeated whole-paper review.

## Output For Storyline Planning

When storyline planning or diagnosis itself is requested, explain the proposed argument in the user's format. Include the scientific question, the insight or construction, how the method follows, what the available findings establish, and the intended section relationships. Mention a material unresolved decision only when one exists.

For ordinary drafting, return the manuscript. Do not expose internal labels, claim-status tables, reviewer simulations, or verification narratives as scientific prose.
